# Design Process

## 1. Context

SOMA FIELD explores how a digital interface might help an older adult experiencing cognitive decline approach a sensory cue without first learning a conventional application structure.

The direction draws on field-visit material from elder-care environments, including familiar objects, sound, changing light, textile making, spatial access and domestic gestures. An anonymised composite narrative, *Flower Aunt*, connects those observations to the possibility that practised bodily gestures can remain meaningful when explicit recall becomes difficult. These materials provide design context; they do not prove that the current interface has been validated with older adults.

## 2. Design question

How might an interface reduce instruction and support recognition through sensory qualities that remain available when abstract navigation becomes difficult?

## 3. Principles

- present one invitation and one primary action at a time
- allow a person to pause, stop or leave without penalty
- keep sound optional and off by default
- use motion to communicate presence and response
- avoid scoring, diagnosis and memory testing
- treat the screen as a threshold into material and spatial experience

## 4. Concept development

Early exploration was organised around environmental atmosphere, emotionally resonant objects and human-centred interactive devices. Field photographs and authored spatial drawings were grouped by recurring sensory qualities rather than presented as clinical evidence. The concept was then narrowed into three complementary encounters:

- **Listen · Window** uses rain, window light, leaf shadows and warmth.
- **Look · Cloth** uses faded yellow cotton and the bodily curve of holding.
- **Touch · Thread** follows the hand through loose thread, loops, crossings and an unfinished resting state.

The three scenes share a trace system so a sensory encounter can be revisited without becoming a score, behavioural record or test result. A returning visit offers the saved colour, word and nearby cue as an invitation rather than asking the person to remember an earlier choice.

## 5. Prototype decisions

- large touch areas and restrained hierarchy
- a three-step path of choose, notice and keep
- short prompts written as invitations rather than commands
- scene-specific touch responses with immediate visible feedback
- sound controls that never autoplay
- persistent but removable traces
- a reduced-motion state that preserves visible artwork
- companion guidance separated from the resident-facing path

## 6. Current evaluation boundary

The current portfolio snapshot has been checked at 390 × 844 and 1440 × 900 for layout, interaction continuity, reduced-motion visibility, default sound state and console stability. Keyboard and screen-reader semantics are included for scene choices, trace selections and modal guidance. This technical review does not replace usability research with older adults, caregivers or clinicians.

The field material is presented with identifying details removed. The narrative is an anonymised composite, and saved traces remain on the current device as preferences for a later invitation rather than clinical evidence.
